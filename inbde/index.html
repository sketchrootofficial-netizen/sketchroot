INBDE preparation guide

The INBDE, the Integrated National Board Dental Examination, is the written examination administered by the Joint Commission on National Dental Examinations (JCNDE) that US dental boards use in licensure decisions. It replaced NBDE Part I and Part II with a single integrated examination of 500 multiple choice items over two days, built around patient cases rather than isolated facts.

By the SketchRoot team, founded by Dr. Jishnu Mohan (doctorj.in). Last reviewed 19 September 2026.

Key facts

Administered by
Joint Commission on National Dental Examinations (JCNDE), part of the American Dental Association
Replaced
NBDE Part I and NBDE Part II
Format
500 multiple choice items delivered by computer over two days at a test centre
Total time
12 hours and 30 minutes of administration time, including the optional tutorial, scheduled breaks and the post exam survey
Scheduling rule
Both days at the same test centre, with the second day within seven days of the first
Scoring
Reported on a scale of 49 to 99, with 75 as the passing standard
Registration
Through JCNDE with a DENTPIN, then scheduling with Prometric

Who takes the INBDE

US dental students usually take it around the third year of dental school. Internationally trained dentists take it as part of the route to US licensure or to advanced standing programmes. Requirements differ by state and by programme, and international candidates must also meet JCNDE eligibility rules, so read the current candidate guide before you plan a date.

What the INBDE tests

JCNDE frames the content as clinical content areas crossed with ten foundation knowledge areas. In practice that means diagnosis, treatment planning, oral health management, ethics and patient safety, together with the science underneath each decision, presented in one clinical frame.

Many items are case based, with a patient history, dental chart, radiographs and medications shown together. Practise reading a patient case in a fixed routine so you do not miss a detail under time pressure.

How the INBDE differs from NEET MDS and INI CET

  • It tests licensure readiness, not ranking. There is a pass standard rather than a merit list.
  • It is far longer: two days and 500 items, so stamina and break management matter.
  • It is case based and integrated, where NEET MDS leans on fact recall and INI CET leans clinical.
  • US standards of care, regulations and terminology apply, which can differ from what an Indian BDS curriculum emphasised.

A study plan structure

  1. Map your gaps against the JCNDE content areas before you start.
  2. Rebuild foundation knowledge through integrated topics, not separate subject silos.
  3. Practise patient case sets under timed conditions.
  4. Rehearse a full two day schedule at least once so stamina is not a surprise.
  5. Use spaced review for the topics you keep missing.

Frequently asked questions

What does INBDE stand for?

INBDE stands for the Integrated National Board Dental Examination.

How many questions are on the INBDE?

The INBDE has 500 multiple choice items, delivered over two days.

How long is the INBDE?

Total administration time is 12 hours and 30 minutes across two days, including the optional tutorial, scheduled breaks and the post exam survey.

What score do you need to pass the INBDE?

Results are reported on a scale of 49 to 99, and 75 is the passing standard.

Did the INBDE replace the NBDE?

Yes. The INBDE replaced NBDE Part I and Part II with one integrated examination.

Can Indian dentists take the INBDE?

Internationally trained dentists can take the INBDE if they meet JCNDE eligibility requirements, which include documentation and credential evaluation. Check the current candidate guide because rules and fees change.
