# Ramu's Kada: Ramus Osteotomies
### Narration script (voice-led, SketchRoot style) · about 8–9 minutes at a relaxed pace

**How to read the cues**
- `[ADD: x]`: that character paints into the scene (pencil → colour → detail, about 4 s). Start the sentence after the cue; the drawing builds while you talk.
- `[CAM: …]`: where the camera drifts.
- `[ARROW: …]` / `[TAG: …]`: a quick red arrow or a small label.
- `[BOX]`: a summary card at the end of a section. Keep talking over it or pause.
- `(pause)`: a 1-second breath. Pauses are where memory forms. Don't rush them.

**Recording tips:** use a quiet room with soft furnishings and a phone held 15–20 cm away. Smile while you talk; it's audible. Record **one section per file** (Intro, Stall 1, Stall 2…), so a mistake costs you one section, not the whole take.

---

## INTRO (about 45 s)
`[TITLE CARD: "Ramu's Kada — featuring the Ramus Osteotomies"]`
`[CAM: wide on the empty shop]`

Welcome to Ramu's Kada: *provisions for better faces*. (pause)

Right now the shop is empty. But by the end of this video, every person standing at this counter will be a surgeon, and every strange thing they're doing will be the operation they gave us.

Quick context before we start. Orthognathic surgery means moving the jaws to correct a dentofacial deformity: a Class II, a Class III, an open bite, an asymmetry. When braces alone can't hide a skeletal problem, we cut the bone and move it. (pause)

And the most famous place we cut the lower jaw is the **ramus**, the vertical back part of the mandible.

`[ADD: Ramu]` `[CAM: push in on Ramu]`

So meet Ramu. His name is literally *Ramus*. This is his shop, and his customers arrive in the order history happened. Let's open up.

## STALL 1 · 1849 · HULLIGAN (about 50 s)
`[CAM: pan left to the first stall]` `[ADD: Hulligan]`

Our first customer is a bit of a hooligan: **Hulligan**. He gives us **Hullihen**, and the year on his board is **1849**.

`[ARROW: the rope around his neck]`

Look at the rope tangled round his neck. Hullihen's patient had a **burn-scar contracture** that had pulled the lower front of the jaw out of place. And Hulligan is sawing himself free.

`[TAG: "anterior alveolar segment"]`

That saw cut is history's first recorded mandibular osteotomy. Hullihen cut and set back a **protruded segment of the anterior mandible**, the front tooth-bearing part. (pause)

Remember: Hulligan, the hooligan in a rope, 1849, the very first cut. *(Some textbooks write 1846 or 1848, but you'll most often see 1849.)*

`[BOX: 1849 · Hullihen · first mandibular osteotomy · anterior alveolar segment]`

---

## STALL 2 · 1907 · BLAIR THE LIAR (about 55 s)
`[CAM: drift right]` `[ADD: Blair]`

Next to him stands a man with a very long nose. His T-shirt says it all: **Blair the Liar**. This is **Blair, 1907**.

`[ARROW: the two wooden blocks]`

Blair has a single plank of wood, and he's cut it **straight across** into two blocks. That's his operation: a **horizontal osteotomy of the ramus**, done from **outside** the mouth, cutting straight across the ramus **above the lingula**.

Simple idea. But why is he a liar? Because the cut *promised* more than it delivered. (pause)

A straight horizontal cut leaves only a **small area of bone touching bone**, and the muscles pull on the fragments. So it **relapsed** a lot, and the nerve was at risk. Today it's history.

`[BOX: 1907 · Blair · horizontal ramus cut (external) · small contact → relapse]`

---

## STALL 3 · 1942 · THE CAT IN THE BOOT (about 55 s)
`[CAM: drift right to the steps]` `[ADD: Cat]`

Now a cat, and not just any cat. She's wearing **boots** and holding up a **chart**.

Boot... chart... **shoe-chart**: **Schuchardt**, **1942**.

`[ARROW: the zig-zag on the chart]` `[ARROW: her feet on the steps]`

Look at what's drawn on her chart: a **step**. And she's literally climbing **steps**. Schuchardt took Blair's straight cut and turned it into a **step**. The cut on the inside of the ramus and the cut on the outside are made at **different heights**, then joined.

Why bother? More overlap, so **more bone contact** than Blair's straight cut.

And remember one more first here: Schuchardt is credited with the **first intraoral approach** to the ramus, operating through the mouth.

`[BOX: 1942 · Schuchardt · first intraoral ramus approach · step cut → more contact]`

---

## STALL 4 · 1957 · HUGO & TRAUMA (about 75 s)
`[CAM: centre, push in]` `[ADD: Hugo & Trauma]`

And now the heart of the shop. A man called **Hugo**, bent double, carrying a huge **water geyser** on his back, with a man in an apron labelled **Trauma** helping him lift it.

Hugo and Trauma: **Hugo Obwegeser** and **Richard Trauner**. Their landmark paper is **1957**.

`[ARROW: the geyser split down the middle]`

Look closely at the geyser. It's **split right down the middle**, front half and back half, with the copper pipes running inside. Ob-we-*geyser*. (pause)

That's the **sagittal split**. Instead of chopping the ramus across, they **split it lengthwise** into an inner and an outer plate. The inner plate carries the tooth-bearing part, and the two plates **slide against each other**.

`[TAG: "broad bone contact"]`

Why is that brilliant? Because the two halves overlap over a **broad surface**. Lots of bone touching bone means good healing. You can **advance** the jaw or **set it back**. And it's all done **through the mouth, with no facial scar**.

*(Obwegeser had first described the split earlier, around 1953 to 1955. The 1957 paper with Trauner is the one everyone remembers.)*

`[BOX: 1957 · Trauner & Obwegeser · sagittal split · broad contact · advance or set back · intraoral]`

---

## STALL 5 · 1961 · DAL (about 55 s)
`[CAM: drift right]` `[ADD: Dal]`

Next, a lady hugging a big sack of **DAL**. And there's a metal **pipe** running down from the sack that **bends sharply downward**.

Dal... **Dal Pont**, **1961**.

`[ARROW: the bend in the pipe]`

That bend is the whole story. Dal Pont changed the cut on the **outer side** of the jaw: instead of running back towards the angle, it turns **straight down** the cheek side of the body, a **vertical buccal cut between the first and second molars**.

Moving that cut forward gives an **even bigger contact surface**, allows **larger advancements**, and needs **less muscle detachment**. (pause)

Dal, the pipe that bends down, the vertical buccal cut.

`[BOX: 1961 · Dal Pont · vertical buccal cut (1st–2nd molar) · bigger contact · bigger advancement]`

---

## STALL 6 · 1968 · THE HEN (about 50 s)
`[CAM: drift right to the glass]` `[ADD: Hen]`

Now a **hen**, sucking on a straw. A hen... sucking... **Hunsuck**. The tag round her neck says **68**: **1968**.

`[ARROW: the straw]`

But look at her straw. It's **short**. Hunsuck **shortened the cut on the inner side of the ramus**. Instead of running all the way to the back border, it stops **just behind the lingula**.

A shorter inner cut means **less soft-tissue stripping** on the inside of the jaw. (pause)

Hen, sucking a short straw: Hunsuck, a short medial cut. *(You may also see this dated 1967.)*

`[BOX: 1968 · Hunsuck · short medial cut, just behind the lingula · less dissection]`

---

## STALL 7 · 1977 · ABKARI (about 50 s)
`[CAM: drift right]` `[ADD: Abkari]`

Last at the counter is a very careful man in a vest. His sign says **ABKARI**. Abkari... **Epker**, **1977**.

`[ARROW: his small knife]`

See how delicately he's slicing, with a tiny knife and no mess. Epker made the split **gentler and more biological**: **minimal stripping of the muscles**, the masseter on the outside and the medial pterygoid on the inside.

Less stripping means **better blood supply, less swelling, and kinder to the muscles and the jaw joint**.

`[BOX: 1977 · Epker · minimal muscle stripping · more biological]`

---

## RECAP: TODAY'S SAGITTAL SPLIT (about 60 s)
`[CAM: slow pull back to show the whole counter]`

Now step back and look at the whole shop. (pause)

The split you'll see in theatre today, the **bilateral sagittal split osteotomy (BSSO)**, is really **four of these customers working together**.
`[ARROW: Hugo]` Hugo gives us the **split**.
`[ARROW: Dal]` Dal gives us the **vertical buccal cut**.
`[ARROW: Hen]` The hen gives us the **short medial cut**.
`[ARROW: Abkari]` And Abkari makes it all **gentle**.

`[BOX: BSSO today — PROS: advance or set back · broad contact · rigid fixation, early function]`

And what can go wrong? Think about what runs right through the middle of Hugo's geyser: those copper pipes. That's your **inferior alveolar nerve**, inside the bone you're splitting. The most common problem after a BSSO is **numbness of the lower lip and chin**. You'll also hear about a **bad split** (the bone cracking the wrong way), **condylar sag**, **relapse**, and **bleeding**.

`[BOX: BSSO — CONS: IAN numbness (most common) · bad split · condylar sag · relapse · bleeding]`
